## 1. MECE & Logic Trees

### What is MECE?

**MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)**:
- Mutually Exclusive: No overlap
- Collectively Exhaustive: No gaps

### MECE Breakdown Patterns

#### Pattern 1: Opposing Concepts
```
Total
├─ Internal / External
├─ Quantitative / Qualitative
├─ Short-term / Long-term
└─ Hard / Soft
```

#### Pattern 2: Process
```
Total
├─ Input
├─ Process
└─ Output
```

#### Pattern 3: Segments
```
Revenue
├─ Product A
├─ Product B
└─ Product C
```

#### Pattern 4: Formula
```
Profit = Revenue - Cost
├─ Revenue = Price × Volume
└─ Cost = Fixed + Variable
```

### Logic Tree Types

#### What Tree (Problem Identification)
```
Revenue Decrease
├─ Volume Decrease
│  ├─ New Customer Decrease
│  └─ Customer Churn
└─ Price Decrease
   ├─ Price Cuts
   └─ Product Mix Change
```

#### Why Tree (Root Cause Analysis)
```
Why did revenue decrease?
├─ Why did new customers decrease?
│  ├─ Advertising effectiveness declined
│  └─ Market awareness decreased
└─ Why did customers churn?
   ├─ Product quality issues
   └─ Service problems
```

#### How Tree (Solution)
```
How to increase revenue?
├─ Acquire new customers
│  ├─ Strengthen advertising
│  └─ Develop new channels
└─ Retain existing customers
   ├─ Improve quality
   └─ Enhance service
```

## 2. 3C Analysis

Analyze market/business from three perspectives: Customer, Competitor, Company.

### 3C Structure

```
┌─────────────┐
│   Customer   │ ← Market
│   (Market)   │
└──────┬──────┘
       │
   ┌───┴───┐
   │       │
┌──▼────┐ ┌▼─────┐
│Company│ │Competitor│
└───────┘ └──────┘
```

### Customer (Market)

#### Analysis Items
- **Market Size**: Current size, growth rate
- **Customer Segments**: Age, gender, income, region
- **Customer Needs**: What they seek
- **Purchase Behavior**: When, where, how they buy
- **Market Trends**: Future predictions

#### Data Collection Example
```
- Market Size: $50B (5% annual growth)
- Primary Customers: Males 30-40, household income $60K+
- Needs: Quality focus, convenience, sustainability
- Purchase Channels: Online 60%, Store 40%
- Trends: Increasing sustainability interest
```

### Competitor

#### Analysis Items
- **Competitors**: Identify key competitors
- **Market Share**: Each company's share
- **Strengths/Weaknesses**: Product, price, brand, channel
- **Strategy**: What strategies they pursue
- **Trends**: Recent moves, future predictions

#### Competitive Comparison Table
| Item | Our Company | Competitor A | Competitor B |
|------|-------------|--------------|--------------|
| Share | 25% | 30% | 20% |
| Price Range | Mid-High | Low-Mid | High |
| Product | ★★★★☆ | ★★★☆☆ | ★★★★★ |
| Brand | ★★★☆☆ | ★★★★☆ | ★★★★★ |
| Channel | Store-focused | Online | Both strong |

### Company (Ourselves)

#### Analysis Items
- **Resources**: People, assets, capital, information
- **Core Competencies**: Unique capabilities
- **Strengths/Weaknesses**: Compared to competitors
- **Current Strategy**: Strategies being pursued
- **Challenges**: Issues to resolve

#### Self-Assessment
```
【Strengths】
- Technology (3 patents held)
- Customer service (85% satisfaction)
- Existing customer base (100K companies)

【Weaknesses】
- Weak online sales (10% share)
- Low brand awareness (30% recognition)
- Lack of price competitiveness (+15% vs. competitors)
```

### 3C Integrated Analysis

Integrate all three to derive opportunities and strategies:

```
【Opportunity】
- Customer: Growing sustainability orientation
- Competitor: Slow to adapt to online
- Company: Technology for eco-friendly products

【Strategy】
Launch eco-friendly products online,
Capture sustainability-oriented customers
```

## 3. 5C Analysis

3C + Collaborator + Context for comprehensive analysis.

### 5C Structure

1. **Customer** ← Same as 3C
2. **Competitor** ← Same as 3C
3. **Company** ← Same as 3C
4. **Collaborator**
5. **Context**

### Collaborator

#### Analysis Items
- **Suppliers**: Raw material/parts suppliers
- **Distributors**: Sales channel partners
- **Partners**: Joint development/sales partners
- **External Partners**: Consultants, agencies, etc.

#### Analysis Perspectives
```
- Dependency: How dependent are we?
- Bargaining Power: Who has stronger negotiation power?
- Substitutability: Are there alternatives?
- Relationship: Good? Room for improvement?
```

### Context (Competitive Environment)

#### PEST Analysis Structure

**Political**:
- Regulatory/legal changes
- Policy directions
- Subsidies/tax systems

**Economic**:
- Economic growth rate
- Exchange rates
- Interest rate trends

**Social**:
- Demographics
- Lifestyle changes
- Value shifts

**Technological**:
- New technology emergence
- Digitalization
- Innovation

## 4. 4P Analysis

Marketing mix analysis across four elements.

### 4P Structure

1. **Product**
2. **Price**
3. **Place** (Distribution)
4. **Promotion**

### Product

#### Analysis Items
- Product lineup
- Quality & features
- Design
- Brand
- Packaging
- After-sales service

### Price

#### Pricing Strategies
- **Premium Pricing**: High quality, high price
- **Penetration Pricing**: Low price for market share
- **Competitive Pricing**: Match competitors
- **Dynamic Pricing**: Adjust based on demand

#### Pricing Methods
```
1. Cost-Based
   Price = Cost × (1 + Margin%)

2. Competition-Based
   Price = Competitor Price ± Differentiation

3. Value-Based
   Price = Customer Perceived Value
```

### Place (Distribution)

#### Channel Strategies
- **Direct Sales**: Own EC, flagship stores
- **Indirect Sales**: Retailers, distributors
- **Omnichannel**: Online-offline integration

### Promotion

#### Promotion Methods
- **Advertising**: TV, web, print, magazines
- **PR**: Press releases, media coverage
- **Sales Promotion**: Discounts, coupons
- **Personal Selling**: Sales activities
- **Social Media Marketing**: Instagram, Twitter, YouTube

## 5. BCG Matrix

Classify business portfolio by market growth rate and relative market share.

### BCG Matrix Structure

```
      Market Growth Rate (High)
           ↑
    ┌──────┬──────┐
    │Question│ Star │
    │   ?   │  ★   │
    ├──────┼──────┤
    │  Dog  │Cash Cow│
    │   ×   │   $   │
    └──────┴──────┘
          → Relative Market Share (High)
```

### Four Quadrants

#### Stars
**Characteristics**:
- High growth market, high share
- Requires investment but generates large revenue

**Strategy**:
- Invest aggressively to maintain/expand share
- Prepare for market growth slowdown

#### Cash Cows
**Characteristics**:
- Low growth market, high share
- Stable revenue source

**Strategy**:
- Minimize investment, harvest profits
- Invest profits in other businesses

#### Question Marks
**Characteristics**:
- High growth market, low share
- Requires investment, generates little profit

**Strategy**:
- Decide: invest to grow or withdraw
- Grow into "Star" or exit

#### Dogs
**Characteristics**:
- Low growth market, low share
- Low profitability and future potential

**Strategy**:
- Withdraw or downsize
- Reallocate resources to other businesses

## 6. Porter's 5 Forces

Analyze industry structure and competitive intensity.

### Five Forces

1. **Threat of New Entrants**
2. **Bargaining Power of Suppliers**
3. **Bargaining Power of Buyers**
4. **Threat of Substitutes**
5. **Rivalry Among Existing Competitors**

### Analysis Framework

#### 1. Threat of New Entrants
- Entry barriers: Capital requirements, regulations, economies of scale
- Brand loyalty
- Access to distribution channels

#### 2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers
- Number of suppliers
- Uniqueness of inputs
- Switching costs

#### 3. Bargaining Power of Buyers
- Buyer concentration
- Price sensitivity
- Availability of alternatives

#### 4. Threat of Substitutes
- Alternative products/services
- Price-performance ratio of substitutes
- Switching costs

#### 5. Competitive Rivalry
- Number of competitors
- Industry growth rate
- Product differentiation
- Exit barriers

## 7. Value Chain

Analyze activities that create value in the business.

### Value Chain Structure

**Primary Activities**:
1. Inbound Logistics
2. Operations
3. Outbound Logistics
4. Marketing & Sales
5. Service

**Support Activities**:
1. Firm Infrastructure
2. Human Resource Management
3. Technology Development
4. Procurement

### Analysis Steps

1. **Identify Activities**: List all value-creating activities
2. **Assess Costs**: Determine cost for each activity
3. **Identify Differentiators**: Where do we create unique value?
4. **Competitive Analysis**: Compare with competitors
5. **Improvement Opportunities**: Where can we improve?

## 8. McKinsey 7S

Holistic organizational analysis framework.

### Seven Elements

**Hard Elements**:
1. **Strategy**: Plan to achieve competitive advantage
2. **Structure**: Organizational structure
3. **Systems**: Processes and procedures

**Soft Elements**:
4. **Shared Values**: Core values and culture
5. **Skills**: Distinctive capabilities
6. **Style**: Leadership style
7. **Staff**: People and their capabilities

### Analysis Approach

- All seven elements must be aligned
- Soft elements are harder to change but critical for success
- Use for organizational change initiatives

## 9. P&L Analysis

Profit and loss statement analysis for financial performance.

### P&L Structure

```
Revenue (売上)
- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
= Gross Profit
- Operating Expenses
  ├─ Selling, General & Administrative (SG&A)
  ├─ Research & Development (R&D)
  └─ Depreciation
= Operating Profit (EBIT)
- Interest & Taxes
= Net Profit
```

### Key Metrics

- **Gross Margin** = Gross Profit / Revenue
- **Operating Margin** = Operating Profit / Revenue
- **Net Margin** = Net Profit / Revenue
- **EBITDA** = EBIT + Depreciation + Amortization

## 10. SWOT Analysis

Analyze internal strengths/weaknesses and external opportunities/threats.

### SWOT Matrix

```
           Internal         External
Positive │ Strengths    │ Opportunities │
Negative │ Weaknesses   │ Threats       │
```

### Analysis Steps

1. **Identify Strengths**: What do we do well?
2. **Identify Weaknesses**: Where do we lack?
3. **Identify Opportunities**: External favorable factors?
4. **Identify Threats**: External unfavorable factors?
5. **Strategy Formulation**: Match strengths to opportunities

### Strategy Matrix

- **SO Strategy**: Use strengths to capitalize on opportunities
- **WO Strategy**: Overcome weaknesses to pursue opportunities
- **ST Strategy**: Use strengths to avoid threats
- **WT Strategy**: Minimize weaknesses and avoid threats

## 11. STP Analysis

Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning for marketing strategy.

### STP Process

1. **Segmentation**: Divide market into segments
2. **Targeting**: Select target segments
3. **Positioning**: Define unique position in minds of customers

### Segmentation Variables

**Geographic**: Region, city size, climate
**Demographic**: Age, gender, income, education
**Psychographic**: Lifestyle, values, personality
**Behavioral**: Usage rate, loyalty, benefits sought

## 12. Business Model Canvas

Visual template for developing business models.

### Nine Building Blocks

1. **Customer Segments**: Who are we serving?
2. **Value Propositions**: What value do we deliver?
3. **Channels**: How do we reach customers?
4. **Customer Relationships**: How do we interact?
5. **Revenue Streams**: How do we earn money?
6. **Key Resources**: What assets do we need?
7. **Key Activities**: What do we do?
8. **Key Partnerships**: Who helps us?
9. **Cost Structure**: What are our costs?

### Canvas Layout


## Best Practices

### When Using Frameworks

1. **Select Appropriate Framework**: Match to business challenge
2. **Gather Quality Data**: Use reliable sources
3. **Be MECE**: Ensure comprehensive, non-overlapping analysis
4. **Derive Insights**: Don't just describe, provide So What
5. **Action-Oriented**: Translate insights to actionable recommendations
6. **Iterate**: Refine analysis based on findings

### Common Pitfalls

- Using frameworks mechanically without thinking
- Collecting data without purpose
- Analysis without insights
- Insights without actions
- Over-complicating simple problems
