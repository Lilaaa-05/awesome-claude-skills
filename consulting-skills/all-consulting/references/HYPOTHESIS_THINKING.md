# Hypothesis Thinking: Theory and Practice

## What is Hypothesis Thinking?

Hypothesis thinking is a problem-solving approach where you form provisional answers (hypotheses) early and then systematically test them, rather than collecting all possible data first.

## Why Hypothesis Thinking?

### Benefits

**Efficiency**:
- Focus data collection on what matters
- Avoid analysis paralysis
- Faster to insight

**Effectiveness**:
- Drive toward actionable answers
- Maintain strategic focus
- Enable iterative refinement

**Clarity**:
- Clear direction for analysis
- Easier team alignment
- Better communication

## The Hypothesis Thinking Process

### 1. Form Initial Hypothesis

Based on:
- Initial information
- Industry knowledge
- Similar cases
- First principles

**Example**:
- Question: "Should we enter market X?"
- Hypothesis: "Yes, we should enter because market is growing rapidly and we have unique technology advantage"

### 2. Identify What to Test

Break hypothesis into testable components:

```
Hypothesis: Should enter market X

Test 1: Is market growing rapidly?
├─ Data needed: Market size trends
└─ Success criteria: >10% annual growth

Test 2: Do we have technology advantage?
├─ Data needed: Competitive tech comparison
└─ Success criteria: 2+ years ahead of competitors

Test 3: Can we capture market share?
├─ Data needed: Customer needs, competitive response
└─ Success criteria: Can achieve 15% share in 3 years
```

### 3. Collect Targeted Data

Focus on:
- Data that proves/disproves hypothesis
- Key decision criteria
- Critical assumptions

Avoid:
- Nice-to-know information
- Comprehensive data dumps
- Tangential analysis

### 4. Evaluate Results

**If Confirmed**:
- Strengthen hypothesis
- Develop detailed plan
- Identify implementation risks

**If Partially Confirmed**:
- Refine hypothesis
- Test revised version
- Adjust strategy

**If Contradicted**:
- Reject hypothesis
- Form alternative hypothesis
- Test new direction

### 5. Iterate

- Refine based on findings
- Test refined hypothesis
- Repeat until confident

## Types of Hypotheses

### Answer Hypothesis
- Direct answer to core question
- "We should enter market X"
- "Price should be reduced by 15%"

### Insight Hypothesis
- Explanation of situation
- "Revenue declined because customer needs shifted"
- "Competitors are vulnerable in segment Y"

### Method Hypothesis
- How to approach problem
- "Customer segmentation by usage pattern will reveal opportunities"
- "Value chain analysis will identify cost reduction areas"

## Hypothesis Formation Techniques

### Issue-Based Hypothesis

Start with structured issue tree:
```
Should we reduce price?
├─ Will it increase volume? → Hypothesis: Yes, by 30%
├─ Will margin remain acceptable? → Hypothesis: Yes, costs will decrease
└─ Will competitors follow? → Hypothesis: No, they have higher costs
```

### Comparative Hypothesis

Compare options:
```
Option A: Enter through acquisition
- Pros: Fast, established presence
- Cons: High cost, integration risk

Option B: Organic entry
- Pros: Lower risk, controlled growth
- Cons: Slow, uncertain success

Hypothesis: Option A is better because speed is critical
```

### Scenario-Based Hypothesis

Consider different futures:
```
Scenario 1: High growth → Strategy: Invest aggressively
Scenario 2: Moderate growth → Strategy: Phased approach
Scenario 3: Low growth → Strategy: Wait and watch

Hypothesis: Scenario 1 most likely (60% probability)
```

## Best Practices

### Form Strong Hypotheses

**Good Hypothesis Characteristics**:
- Specific and clear
- Testable with data
- Actionable if confirmed
- Based on logic/evidence
- Time-bound when relevant

**Example of Strong Hypothesis**:
"Reducing price by 10% will increase volume by 25% within 6 months, resulting in 15% profit improvement, because price is the primary purchase driver for our target segment (as indicated by customer surveys)"

**Example of Weak Hypothesis**:
"We should probably lower prices to see what happens"

### Structure Testing Plan

For each hypothesis component:
```
Component: [What to test]
├─ Data Source: [Where to get data]
├─ Analysis Method: [How to analyze]
├─ Success Criteria: [What proves/disproves]
└─ Timeline: [When to complete]
```

### Document Assumptions

Make explicit:
- What you're assuming
- Why you're assuming it
- Risk if assumption is wrong
- How to validate

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Confirmation Bias
❌ Only looking for supporting evidence
✅ Actively seek disconfirming evidence

### Mistake 2: Over-Confidence
❌ Treating hypothesis as fact
✅ Maintain appropriate uncertainty

### Mistake 3: Rigidity
❌ Sticking to disproven hypothesis
✅ Willingness to pivot

### Mistake 4: Vagueness
❌ "Customers probably want better service"
✅ "70% of churned customers cited response time >24hrs as key reason"

### Mistake 5: Untestable Hypotheses
❌ "We should be more innovative"
✅ "Launching 3 new products per year will increase revenue by 20%"

## Integration with Frameworks

### Hypothesis + 3C Analysis

```
Hypothesis: We can gain share in segment X

Test through 3C:
├─ Customer: Do segment X customers value our strengths?
├─ Competitor: Are competitors weak in segment X?
└─ Company: Do we have capabilities for segment X?
```

### Hypothesis + SWOT

```
Hypothesis: International expansion is best growth strategy

Test through SWOT:
├─ Strengths: Can our strengths transfer internationally?
├─ Weaknesses: Will our weaknesses be magnified?
├─ Opportunities: Are international opportunities attractive?
└─ Threats: Can we manage international risks?
```

## Example: Complete Hypothesis Cycle

**Initial Hypothesis**:
"We should enter e-commerce channel because 60% of customers now prefer online shopping"

**Testing Plan**:
1. Validate customer preference (customer surveys, industry data)
2. Assess competitive landscape (competitor e-commerce capabilities)
3. Estimate required investment (technology, logistics, marketing)
4. Calculate financial returns (revenue potential, costs, payback period)

**Findings**:
- ✅ Confirmed: 65% prefer online (higher than assumed)
- ✅ Confirmed: Competitors' e-commerce is weak
- ❌ Contradicted: Investment required is 2x initial estimate
- ⚠️ Partially: Margins will be 5% lower than expected

**Revised Hypothesis**:
"We should enter e-commerce with phased approach, starting with high-margin products, because customer demand is strong and competition is weak, but upfront investment is high"

**Next Steps**:
- Develop detailed phased plan
- Identify high-margin product subset
- Finalize investment and P&L
- Create implementation roadmap
